# Getting Your Deposit Back

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** A security deposit is money you pay at the start of a lease that the landlord holds to cover certain costs. This chapter explains what to record at move-in, how the pre-move-out inspection works, what a landlord may deduct, and the rules for returning the deposit. It ends with calm, practical steps if you and the landlord disagree.

## Start at move-in

Good records at move-in are the strongest tool you have at move-out. Before you unpack:

- **Walk through every room.** Use a move-in checklist if the landlord provides one. If not, make your own.
- **Take photos and video.** Capture walls, floors, carpets, windows, appliances (inside and out), cabinets, fixtures, and any existing damage. Get close-ups of scratches, stains, and holes.
- **Keep the files organized.** Name photos by room, and keep the original files so the date information stays with them.
- **Share a copy.** Send your notes and photos to the landlord in writing within the first days, and ask them to add anything they see differently. Keep a copy of what you sent.

Also check the deposit terms in your lease. A lease may not make a security deposit, or any part of it, nonrefundable [F:deposit-no-nonrefundable]. A clause calling part of the deposit "nonrefundable" is worth a closer look [S:civ-1950-5].

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Before you share your lease, photos, or a deposit statement with an AI provider, you may want to redact Social Security numbers, bank details, and signatures. The AI provider's terms govern what happens to what you share.

## The pre-move-out inspection

A tenant may request an initial inspection before moving out, no earlier than two weeks before the tenancy ends. This gives the tenant a chance to fix problems the landlord identifies. The landlord must tell the tenant in writing about this right [F:deposit-pre-moveout-inspection].

Make the request in writing and keep a copy. During the inspection, take notes and ask for a written list of anything the landlord plans to charge for. Then fix or clean what you reasonably can before you leave.

## What a landlord may deduct

A landlord may deduct only what is reasonably necessary for [F:deposit-allowed-deductions]:

- Unpaid rent.
- Repairing damage beyond ordinary wear and tear caused by the tenant or the tenant's guests.
- Cleaning needed to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness.
- If the lease allows it, replacing personal property beyond ordinary wear and tear.

"Ordinary wear and tear" is the normal aging of a home from everyday living.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — LegalFriend describes ordinary wear and tear as: "Reasonable wear and tear from normal use" [F:deposit-allowed-deductions]

Whether a particular item is wear and tear or damage depends on the details. Your move-in photos, move-out photos, and how long you lived there all help show what changed. The official guide has more on this topic [S:dre-california-tenants].

## Move-out: photos and the 21-day rule

Before you return the keys, repeat your move-in walk-through. Photograph the same spots from the same angles. Note the date and time you returned the keys, and give the landlord your new mailing address in writing.

Newer rules require landlords to take photos of the unit at certain points to support deductions. Examples include at move-out before repairs or cleaning, and at move-in for tenancies starting on or after a 2025 effective date [F:deposit-photos]. Check the official source for the exact dates and requirements [S:civ-1950-5].

Within 21 days after the tenant moves out, the landlord must return the deposit or send an itemized statement of deductions with any remaining balance [F:deposit-21-days]. An "itemized statement" lists each deduction separately with its amount.

When deductions for repairs or cleaning total more than $125, the statement must include copies of receipts or invoices. If the work could not be completed within 21 days, the landlord may send a good-faith estimate, followed by the receipts [F:deposit-receipts].

## If you disagree with the deductions

Clear, written communication is the best place to start.

**Step 1: Review the statement.** Compare each deduction to your move-in and move-out photos and notes. Check whether receipts or invoices are included where required [F:deposit-receipts]. Your AI can help you build a simple table: each deduction, the amount, what your records show, and your questions.

**Step 2: Send a written request.** Write a short, polite letter or email. List the deductions you question, explain why using your records, attach key photos, and ask for the amount you believe should be returned by a specific date. Keep the tone neutral and factual. Keep a copy.

**Step 3: Know what the law provides.** If a landlord keeps a deposit in bad faith, a court may award statutory damages of up to twice the amount of the deposit, in addition to actual damages [F:deposit-bad-faith]. This kit does not define "bad faith" or say when it applies. Check the official source [S:civ-1950-5].

**Step 4: Consider small claims court.** If the written request does not resolve it, small claims court is one option. A natural person may generally sue for up to $12,500 in California small claims court, which is where many deposit disputes are heard [F:small-claims-limit] [S:ccp-116-221]. LegalFriend's California Small Claims Kit walks through filing, serving the other side, and preparing for the hearing. It is a self-help guide and cannot predict how any case will turn out. The Attorney General's landlord-tenant page is another official starting point [S:oag-landlord-tenant].

## Checklist

- [ ] Took dated move-in photos and video of every room and appliance.
- [ ] Sent move-in notes and photos to the landlord in writing and kept a copy.
- [ ] Checked the lease for any "nonrefundable" deposit wording.
- [ ] Requested a pre-move-out inspection in writing (no earlier than two weeks before the end).
- [ ] Fixed or cleaned items identified at the inspection.
- [ ] Took move-out photos from the same angles as move-in.
- [ ] Gave the landlord a forwarding address in writing and noted the key-return date.
- [ ] Counted 21 days from move-out on a calendar.
- [ ] Checked any itemized statement for receipts where deductions total more than $125.
- [ ] If there is a dispute, sent a written request before taking further steps.
