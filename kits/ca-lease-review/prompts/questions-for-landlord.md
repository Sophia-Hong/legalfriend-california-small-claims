# Questions for the landlord

**Sections:** any · **Workflow step:** questions

## When to use
The person has a list of flagged items from the clause check, or parts of the lease they
simply don't understand, and wants to ask the landlord or property manager about them in
writing — before signing, or at any point during the tenancy.

## What to do
1. Collect the items: every row from the clause-check table, plus anything the person
   marked as unclear. Ask the person which ones they actually want to raise. It is their
   choice what to send, and whether to send anything.
2. Ask whether this is **before signing** or **during the tenancy**, and how they normally
   communicate with the landlord (email, portal, letter).
3. Write one short question per item:
   - Quote or point to the clause (page and section) so the reader can find it.
   - Ask to **clarify, confirm, or explain** — for example, "Could you confirm how the
     $___ cleaning deposit is handled at move-out?" or "Could you share how the late fee
     amount was set?"
   - Ask one thing per question. Keep each to one or two sentences.
   - No legal conclusions, no statute numbers, no deadlines or demands, no hints about
     courts or complaints. Do not say a clause is illegal or unenforceable.
   - No guesses about the landlord's motives. Assume a standard form and a good-faith
     reader.
4. Add practical asks that help both sides keep clear records, where they fit:
   - A complete signed copy of the lease and every addendum.
   - A written translation, if the lease was negotiated mainly in Spanish, Chinese,
     Tagalog, Vietnamese, or Korean [F:translation].
   - How to submit repair requests and who to contact in an emergency.
   - How the landlord prefers to schedule entry and give notice [F:entry-notice].
   - Before move-out: how to request the pre-move-out inspection
     [F:deposit-pre-moveout-inspection].
5. Put the questions into a short, polite message: greeting, one sentence of context,
   numbered questions, thanks, and the person's name and unit. Keep the tone friendly and
   matter-of-fact.
6. Remind the person to keep a copy of what they send and any reply, with dates, and to
   ask for important answers in writing. Say that an answer from the landlord is not the
   same as a change to the lease; if a term changes, they may ask for a written, signed
   addendum.
7. Do not send anything yourself.
