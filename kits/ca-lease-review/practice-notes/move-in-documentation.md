# Documenting the unit at move-in

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

A clear move-in record takes an hour or two on move-in day and can save a lot of back-and-forth when the tenancy ends. It helps landlords too: a shared, dated record means fewer disagreements about what changed. This note is general information, not advice about any specific lease.

## Why the starting point matters

At move-out, a landlord may deduct from the deposit only for certain things. These include repairing damage beyond ordinary wear and tear caused by the tenant or guests, and cleaning needed to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness. [F:deposit-allowed-deductions] Both depend on what the unit looked like at the start. Without a record, people are left comparing memories.

LegalFriend's standard wording for what is *not* damage is short: "Reasonable wear and tear from normal use". The kit does not define wear and tear beyond that. Whether a specific mark is wear and tear or damage is a judgment call, which is exactly why dated photos of the starting point help.

## What to record

Go room by room in the same order every time. For each room, look at:

- Walls, ceilings, floors or carpet, and baseboards
- Doors, locks, windows, screens, and blinds
- Lights, switches, outlets, and fixtures
- Cabinets, counters, sinks, and appliances (open them and look inside)
- Plumbing, heating, cooling, and smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

Describe what you see in plain words: "two small nail holes above bed." Avoid guesses about who caused it. Note cleanliness as well as condition, since cleaning deductions are measured against the move-in level. [F:deposit-allowed-deductions]

## Photos and video

- Take a wide shot of each room, then close-ups of anything worn, marked, or broken.
- Put a coin or ruler in close-ups for scale.
- Keep the original files. They usually carry date and time information that a screenshot or edited copy may lose.
- Back everything up in two places, and label files by room.

Newer rules require landlords to take photos at certain points, including at move-in for some tenancies, to support deductions. [F:deposit-photos] The exact dates are still being confirmed, and your own record is useful either way.

## Share it in writing

If the landlord gives you a move-in checklist, fill it in carefully and keep a copy. Either way, send your completed record to the landlord in writing and save the message. A short, friendly note works: "Attached is my move-in condition record and photos for Unit 4. Please let me know if you see anything different."

## Problems that affect living conditions

Some things are more than cosmetic. California rental housing must meet basic habitability standards, including working plumbing, hot and cold water, heating, electrical systems, and freedom from rodents and vermin. [F:habitability] LegalFriend's standard wording on this point is: "Despite any contradicting clause, your landlord is still required to maintain heating, plumbing, and a safe structure. Document any issues and ask for repairs in writing."

## Plan ahead for move-out

Put two dates in your calendar now: the lease end date, and a reminder about two weeks before it. A tenant may request an initial inspection before moving out, no earlier than two weeks before the tenancy ends, to get a chance to fix identified problems. [F:deposit-pre-moveout-inspection] Your move-in record is what you will compare against.

## Common pitfalls

- Waiting until boxes are unpacked, when furniture hides floors and walls.
- Photographing only damage, with no wide shots to show the rest was fine.
- Skipping inside appliances, cabinets, and closets.
- Keeping only screenshots or edited copies instead of original files.
- Never sending the record to the landlord, so it was never shared.
- Burying living-condition problems among cosmetic notes instead of asking for repairs in writing. [F:habitability]
