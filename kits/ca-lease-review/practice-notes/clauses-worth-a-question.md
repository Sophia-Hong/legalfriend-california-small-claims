# Clauses worth a question

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

Most leases use standard forms, and most clauses are routine. A few kinds of clauses are worth a closer look. This note lists them, the related fact, and a neutral way to ask about each. It is general information. It does not say whether any clause in your lease is enforceable.

Where LegalFriend has attorney-approved standard wording for a type of clause, it is quoted below exactly as written.

## Deposits, added together

Security, pet, key, and cleaning deposits generally count together toward the limit. [F:deposit-what-counts] For most leases, the total may not be more than one month's rent. [F:deposit-limit-general] A small landlord who fits the definition may collect up to two months' rent [F:deposit-limit-small-landlord], but not when the tenant is a service member. [F:deposit-limit-servicemember]

LegalFriend's standard wording: "All deposits combined (security + pet + other) must comply with CA limits: 1 month rent for most landlords, 2 months for small owners (≤2 properties, ≤4 units)"

Add every deposit and divide by monthly rent. A neutral question: "Could you confirm the total of all deposits and whether the small-landlord rule applies to this property?"

## "Nonrefundable" deposits

A lease may not make a security deposit, or any part of it, nonrefundable. [F:deposit-no-nonrefundable] If a charge is labeled both "deposit" and "nonrefundable," ask what it covers.

## Late fees

A late fee must be a reasonable estimate of the landlord's actual costs from late payment. A fee above about 5% of monthly rent is commonly treated as a signal to look more closely. [F:late-fee-reasonable]

LegalFriend's standard wording: "This late fee is over 5% of rent (California's common "reasonableness" benchmark)—if charged, demand proof of the landlord's actual costs before paying."

Before signing, a gentler question often works: "Could you share how the late fee amount was set?"

## Waivers of basic protections

A clause that has the tenant give up rights to a habitable home, to proper entry notice, to deposit protections, or to hold the landlord responsible for the landlord's own negligence is void. [F:waivers-void] Look for "release," "waive," or "hold harmless" language.

LegalFriend's standard wording: "Despite this clause, landlords remain liable for their negligence and must provide habitable housing."

## Jury waivers

A pre-dispute waiver of the right to a jury trial in a lease is unenforceable in California. [F:jury-waiver]

LegalFriend's standard wording: "Despite this clause, you can still request a jury trial. This waiver is invalid in CA."

## One-way attorney's fees

If a lease says only the landlord can recover attorney's fees, California law makes that clause apply to both sides. [F:attorney-fees-reciprocal]

## Automatic renewal

An automatic renewal clause is voidable by the tenant unless it appears in at least 8-point boldface type immediately above the tenant's signature. [F:auto-renewal] Note how and where it is printed.

## Entry

LegalFriend's standard wording: "California requires reasonable notice (commonly 24+ hours) for non-emergency entry". [F:entry-notice] An "at any time" entry clause is worth a question about notice.

## Translation

If the lease was negotiated mainly in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, or Korean, the landlord must provide a translation before signing. [F:translation]

## Local rules

Many cities and counties have stricter rules than the state. [F:local-rules] Check the rules for the property's address.

## Common pitfalls

- Checking each deposit alone instead of adding them together. [F:deposit-what-counts]
- Assuming the small-landlord rule applies, or doesn't, without asking.
- Treating a "worth a closer look" item as a reason to stop paying rent. Ask in writing instead.
- Reading only the main lease and skipping addenda and house rules.
- Forgetting local rules. [F:local-rules]
