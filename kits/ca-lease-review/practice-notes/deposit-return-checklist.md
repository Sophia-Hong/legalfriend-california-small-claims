# Deposit return checklist

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

Getting a deposit back goes most smoothly when both sides have the same information: a clear move-out date, a forwarding address, and photos of the unit at the start and the end. This checklist walks through the weeks around move-out. It is general information, not advice about any specific tenancy, and it does not predict what anyone will receive.

## About a month before move-out

- Find your move-in record and photos. You will compare against them.
- Re-read the Deposit and Ending sections of your lease, including any cleaning or move-out requirements.
- Give notice the way your lease requires, in writing, and keep a copy.
- Decide whether to request a pre-move-out inspection. A tenant may request an initial inspection before moving out, no earlier than two weeks before the tenancy ends, so the tenant has a chance to fix identified problems. [F:deposit-pre-moveout-inspection] Ask in writing and note the date you asked.

## The last two weeks

- If an inspection happens, write down what was identified and fix what you reasonably can.
- Clean to the level the unit was at when you moved in. That level is the measure for cleaning deductions. [F:deposit-allowed-deductions]
- Repeat your move-in photo log, room by room, after cleaning and before handing back the keys. Keep the original files.
- Newer rules require landlords to take photos at certain points, including at move-out before repairs or cleaning. [F:deposit-photos] Your own photos are still worth taking.

## Move-out day

- Return keys in a way you can show later (a receipt, a photo, or a message saying when and where).
- Give your forwarding address in writing.
- Write down the date. This starts the clock below.

## The 21 days after move-out

Within 21 days after the tenant moves out, the landlord must return the deposit or send an itemized statement of deductions with any remaining balance. [F:deposit-21-days] Count forward 21 days from your move-out date and put that date in your calendar. Confirm the counting on the official source.

## Reading an itemized statement

Go line by line. A landlord may deduct only what is reasonably necessary for unpaid rent, repairing damage beyond ordinary wear and tear caused by the tenant or guests, cleaning needed to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness, and (if the lease allows) replacing personal property beyond ordinary wear and tear. [F:deposit-allowed-deductions] LegalFriend's standard wording for what falls outside damage is: "Reasonable wear and tear from normal use".

For each line, note which category it seems to fit, what your photos show, and whether paperwork is attached. When repair and cleaning deductions total more than $125, the statement must include copies of receipts or invoices, or a good-faith estimate followed by receipts if the work could not be finished in 21 days. [F:deposit-receipts] Also check that no part of the deposit was treated as nonrefundable. [F:deposit-no-nonrefundable]

## Asking about it in writing

If something is missing or unclear, send a short, polite written request. Give your move-out date and forwarding address, list what you paid, and either ask for the balance or itemization, or ask specific questions about specific lines. Offer your move-in record. Keep it factual and calm; a clear question often gets a clear answer.

## If it stays unresolved

Many deposit disputes are heard in small claims court, where a natural person may generally sue for up to $12,500. [F:small-claims-limit] LegalFriend's California Small Claims Kit covers that process. Some cities also have their own rules or programs. [F:local-rules]

## Common pitfalls

- Not giving a forwarding address in writing.
- Taking move-out photos before cleaning instead of after.
- Losing track of the move-out date, so the 21 days can't be counted. [F:deposit-21-days]
- Disputing the whole statement instead of asking about specific lines.
- Skipping the pre-move-out inspection request. [F:deposit-pre-moveout-inspection]
