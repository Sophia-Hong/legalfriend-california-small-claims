# Naming the defendant correctly

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

The "defendant" is the person or business you are suing. Getting the name right is one of the most common places where small claims cases run into trouble later. California Courts warns that naming the defendant incorrectly can prevent the plaintiff from collecting after winning. [F:defendant-name-collection] This note is general issue-spotting. It is not advice about any specific case.

## Start with who you actually dealt with

Before you open the form, write down who you made the deal with, who you paid, and who caused the loss. These are not always the same. A small claims case starts with form SC-100, so this list becomes your starting point for that form. [F:sc100-starts-case] [S:jcc-form-sc-100]

It helps to gather every paper that shows a name: contracts, receipts, invoices, canceled checks, card statements, emails, texts, and business cards.

## Individuals

For a person, people commonly use the full legal name, not a nickname. If you only know part of the name, look at documents you already have, such as a signed agreement or a check. Check the official instructions on how to list a person's name. [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

## Sole proprietors with a business name (DBA)

Many small businesses are one person using a trade name. This is sometimes called a "DBA" ("doing business as") or a fictitious business name. The sign may say one thing while the owner is someone else. Counties keep a fictitious business name index, and many let you search it online or at the county clerk's office. Check the official instructions on how to list both the owner and the business name. [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

If you are the one doing business under a fictitious name, that is a separate issue. A plaintiff doing business under a fictitious business name must file a declaration (form SC-103). [F:fbn-declaration] [S:jcc-form-sc-103]

## LLCs and corporations

For a limited liability company (LLC) or a corporation, the exact registered name matters. Small differences, such as "Inc." versus "LLC," or a missing word, can point to a different business or to no business at all. The California Secretary of State's online business search (bizfile) shows the registered name, the status, and the agent for service of process. Check the official guidance on how to name a business defendant. [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

## Franchises and parent companies

A store with a national brand name is often owned by a separate local company. The brand's parent company may not be the business you dealt with. Receipts, contracts, and posted business licenses can help show the actual owner. Search that owner in bizfile or the county index before relying on the brand name.

## More than one possible defendant

Sometimes more than one person or business may be involved. Form SC-100A lists additional plaintiffs or defendants when SC-100 does not have room for all of them. [F:sc100a] [S:jcc-form-sc-100a] Whether more than one defendant belongs in a case depends on the facts. Each county provides free small claims advisor assistance, and an advisor can talk through the general options. [F:advisors]

If a possible defendant is a public entity, such as a city, county, or school district, a different step usually comes first. A claimant usually must first present a government claim, generally within six months for injury or property damage and one year for other claims. [F:government-claim]

## Common pitfalls

- Using the brand or sign name when the legal owner is a different person or company.
- Leaving out "Inc.," "LLC," or other words that are part of the registered name.
- Listing only the trade name when the business is one person using a DBA.
- Assuming a franchise location is owned by the national company.
- Copying a name from a website or social media without checking bizfile or the county index.
- Not saving a printout or screenshot of the name search, with the date you did it.
- Missing the government claim step when the other side is a public entity. [F:government-claim]

## Where to get help

California Courts offers free form-filling help, including Guide & File, and free small claims advisors. [F:guide-and-file] [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]
