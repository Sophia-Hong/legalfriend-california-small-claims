# Filing

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** You have a completed SC-100. Filing is the step where you give it to the court and the court opens your case. This chapter covers copies, fees, the fee waiver, the ways a court may accept papers, local requirements, and what to check once the clerk has processed your filing.

## What "filing" means

Filing means giving your papers to the court clerk so the court can open a case. The clerk is the court employee who accepts and processes papers. A small claims case starts with form SC-100, *Plaintiff's Claim and ORDER to Go to Small Claims Court* [F:sc100-starts-case].

Before you file, gather everything you prepared in Chapter 6:

- Your signed SC-100.
- SC-100A, if you have more plaintiffs or defendants than SC-100 has room for [F:sc100a].
- SC-103, if you are doing business under a fictitious business name. A plaintiff in that situation must file this declaration showing compliance with the fictitious business name laws [F:fbn-declaration].
- Any local form your court requires (see "Local requirements" below).

## Making copies

Make a full copy of every page you plan to file before you go to court or upload anything. Keep one complete set for your own records.

How many copies the court wants, and whether it wants paper copies at all when you file electronically, is up to the court. Check the official guidance and your court's instructions before you print [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]. If you are unsure, ask the clerk.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — A simple habit helps: keep a "filed" folder with a copy of exactly what you gave the court, in the same page order. When you later arrange service or answer a question at the hearing, you are working from the same pages the court has.

## Filing fees

Small claims filing fees are not one fixed number. They depend on how much you are claiming and how many small claims you have filed in the past 12 months [F:fees-vary]. This kit does not list fee amounts because they can change. Check the court's current fee schedule before you file [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

A related rule: a person may not file more than two small claims cases demanding more than $2,500 anywhere in California in the same calendar year [F:two-claims-rule]. If you have filed other small claims cases, count them before you file.

Ask your court which payment methods it accepts. This can differ from court to court.

## Fee waiver (FW-001)

If you cannot afford the court fees, you can ask for a fee waiver using form FW-001, *Request to Waive Court Fees* [F:fee-waiver]. To "waive" a fee means to excuse you from paying it.

Who qualifies, what the form asks for, and how the court decides are explained in the official form and its instructions. Read them before you fill out the request [S:jcc-form-fw-001]. Ask the clerk how your court wants the fee waiver request submitted along with your SC-100.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — The fee waiver form asks for financial details. Gather your income and expense information before you sit down. The form is easier to finish in one sitting when the numbers are in front of you.

## Electronic filing or in-person filing

Courts do not all accept small claims papers the same way. Depending on the court, options may include filing online, filing at the clerk's window, or filing by mail. This kit does not say which options your court offers. Check your court's website or call the clerk's office [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]. When you check, look for:

- Whether the court accepts small claims filings online, and through which system.
- Whether in-person filing needs an appointment.
- Whether mailed filings are accepted, and where to send them.
- How fees are paid for each method.

California Courts offers free form-filling help, including Guide & File, and free small claims advisors [F:guide-and-file]. Each county provides free small claims advisor assistance [F:advisors]. An advisor can help you understand the filing steps where you live.

## Local requirements

Some courts require additional local forms, so check your specific court's website or ask the clerk [F:local-forms]. A local form is a form created by one county's court, not a statewide Judicial Council form. This kit does not list local forms or local rules for any county.

Before you file, confirm:

- Whether your court has its own small claims forms or cover sheets.
- Whether your court has special instructions for attachments.
- Whether your court has its own filing hours, drop-box procedures, or online portal.

Use the statewide small claims forms list to confirm the current version of each statewide form [S:cc-small-claims-forms]. Make sure you are using the current SC-100 from the official form page [S:jcc-form-sc-100].

## What to check after the clerk processes your filing

The official guidance explains what the clerk gives back to you after filing and how you learn your hearing date. Read it so you know what to expect [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

Once the clerk has processed your papers, make sure you know the details below. If any are missing or unclear, ask the clerk before you leave, or contact the clerk's office:

- Your case number.
- Your hearing date and time.
- The courthouse address and the department or courtroom.
- Whether the court needs anything else from you.

Write the hearing date on your calendar right away. The service deadlines in Chapter 8 are counted back from the hearing date [F:service-deadline].

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Read the filed papers carefully when you get them back. Check that names, addresses, and the amount are exactly what you intended. Catching a typo now is easier than fixing it later.
