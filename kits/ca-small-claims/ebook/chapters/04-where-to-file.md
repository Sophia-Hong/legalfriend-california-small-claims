# Where Can You File?

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** A small claims case has to be filed in a court that the venue rules allow. This chapter explains what "venue" means, the common ideas behind it, and how to find and check the right courthouse. It also explains why picking the court that is closest to you is not, by itself, how venue is decided.

## What "venue" means

"Venue" is the legal word for *which court location* hears a case. It is a separate question from whether small claims is the right kind of court for the dispute. Even when small claims fits, the case still has to be filed in a place the rules permit.

California has specific venue rules for small claims. Commonly, a case may be filed where a defendant lives or does business, where a contract was made or was supposed to be carried out, or where the injury or property damage happened. There are also special rules for certain consumer contracts. [F:venue-basics]

That is a summary, not the full rule. The statute and the laws it points to contain the exact wording, and the details matter. Read the current text on the official source before relying on any one category. [S:ccp-116-370]

## Thinking through the venue categories

It can help to write down the facts that connect the dispute to a place. This is organizing work, not a legal decision. For each item below, note the city and county, and where the information came from.

- **Where each defendant lives.** If there is more than one defendant, list each one separately.
- **Where each business defendant does business.** A business may have more than one location. Note the address you have and how you know it.
- **Where the agreement was made.** For example, where a contract was signed or where an order was placed.
- **Where the agreement was to be carried out.** For example, where the work was to be done or the goods delivered.
- **Where the injury or property damage happened.**
- **Whether the dispute involves a consumer contract.** Special venue rules apply to certain consumer contracts. [F:venue-basics] This kit does not list those special rules. Check the current rule on the official source. [S:ccp-116-370]

Once the list is done, compare it to the official venue rule. Some disputes connect to only one place. Others connect to several. When the facts point to more than one place, or when it is unclear which category fits, that is a question to bring to the official source or a small claims advisor. Each county offers free small claims advisor help. [F:advisors] [S:ccp-116-260]

## Why convenience alone does not decide venue

It is natural to want to file at the courthouse nearest home or work. But venue is set by the rules described above, not by what is easiest for the person filing. [F:venue-basics] A courthouse near the plaintiff may be a proper choice, or it may not be, depending on how the facts line up with the venue categories.

This kit does not state what happens if a case is filed in a place the rules do not allow. For that question, check the current official guidance. [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

> **LegalFriend practice note** — A common mix-up is treating "the court near me" and "the correct court" as the same thing. They sometimes match and sometimes do not. Writing out the venue facts first, before looking at a map, keeps the two questions separate.

## Finding the courthouse

After working through the venue categories, the next step is to find the actual court location that handles small claims for that area.

- **Start on the official California Courts self-help site.** It explains the small claims process step by step and links to court resources. [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]
- **Go to the specific court's own website.** Use the link from the official site rather than a search ad or a third-party directory, so you land on the real court page.
- **Confirm the small claims location.** A county may have several courthouses. Check which one handles small claims for the area you identified, and note its address, filing hours, and any filing options it lists.
- **Write down what you found and the date you checked.** Court web pages change. A dated note helps if you need to look again later.

## Checking local requirements and forms

A small claims case starts with the statewide form SC-100. [F:sc100-starts-case] [S:jcc-form-sc-100] But SC-100 may not be the only paper a court wants. Some courts require extra local forms, so check the specific court's website or ask the clerk. [F:local-forms] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

When you visit the court's website, look for:

- A small claims page or self-help center page.
- A list of local forms, if any, for small claims.
- Any local instructions about how to file, what to bring, or how many copies are needed.
- Contact details for the clerk's office and the small claims advisor.

This kit does not list local rules or local forms for any county. The court's own website and clerk are the sources for those.

California Courts also offers free help with forms, including Guide & File, along with free small claims advisors. [F:guide-and-file] [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

> **LegalFriend practice note** — It helps to save or print the court's small claims page on the day you check it. If a question comes up later about what the court asked for, you will have a dated record of what the page said.
