# Judgment

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** A "judgment" is the court's final decision in your case. This chapter explains the notice the court sends, who can appeal, why there is a waiting period before collection, and the basic tools the official sources describe for collecting. It ends with what to file once a judgment is paid in full.

## Understanding the result

A judgment says who owes money to whom, and how much. It may be for the full amount you asked for, part of it, or none of it. If the defendant filed a claim against you, the judgment will also address that claim.

Two terms come up often after a judgment:

- **Judgment creditor** — the party the judgment says is owed money.
- **Judgment debtor** — the party the judgment says must pay.

Read the judgment carefully. Check that the names, the amount, and the case number are correct. If something looks wrong or unclear, check the official source for what options may exist and how to contact the court [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

## The Notice of Entry of Judgment

The clerk sends the parties a Notice of Entry of Judgment (form SC-130) [F:notice-of-entry]. Keep it with your case file. The date it was mailed or delivered matters, because the appeal deadline counts from that date (see below).

## Who can appeal

An appeal is a request for a higher level of review. In small claims, the rules are different for each side:

- A plaintiff cannot appeal a judgment on the plaintiff's own claim [F:plaintiff-no-appeal].
- A defendant can appeal. That leads to a new hearing in superior court [F:plaintiff-no-appeal].
- A plaintiff can appeal a judgment on a defendant's claim [F:plaintiff-no-appeal].

A notice of appeal (form SC-140) generally must be filed within 30 days after the clerk mails or delivers the notice of entry of judgment [F:appeal-deadline].

Appeals are outside the main scope of this kit. If the defendant appeals, or you are thinking about appealing a judgment on a defendant's claim, check the official source for the full process, fees, and what the new hearing involves [S:jcc-form-sc-140] [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]. Other post-judgment requests, such as asking the court to vacate (cancel) a judgment, also have their own rules. Check the official source for those [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

## Waiting before collection

The party who is owed money generally must wait until the time to appeal, or to ask the court to vacate the judgment, has passed before starting collection [F:enforcement-wait]. For how to count that waiting period in your situation, check the official source [S:ccp-116-810].

Some people pay on their own once a judgment is entered. For how payment can be made, and whether the debtor can ask to pay over time, check the official source [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Keep a simple payment log: date, amount, how it was paid, and any receipt. A clear record makes it easier to confirm the balance and to complete the right form once the judgment is paid.

## The Statement of Assets

Unless the judgment is paid or appealed, the judgment debtor generally must complete a Judgment Debtor's Statement of Assets (form SC-133) and send it to the judgment creditor within the time the law sets [F:statement-of-assets]. As its name suggests, the form is about the debtor's assets. For what the form asks, the exact deadline, and what can be done if the form is not received, check the official source [S:ccp-116-830] [S:jcc-form-sc-133].

## Collection tools: an overview

If a judgment is not paid, the official sources describe several collection tools. Common ones include:

- **Writ of execution (form EJ-130).** A court document used for bank levies and wage garnishment [F:enforcement-tools]. A "levy" means taking money from an account to pay the judgment. "Wage garnishment" means taking part of a paycheck.
- **Abstract of judgment (form EJ-001).** A document that creates a lien on real property [F:enforcement-tools]. A "lien" is a legal claim attached to property, such as a house or land.
- **Order to appear for examination.** An order for the debtor to appear for an examination [F:enforcement-tools]. For what this involves, check the official source [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

Each tool has its own forms, steps, and possible fees. There may also be limits on what can be collected. This kit does not cover those details. Check the official source and the form pages before using any tool [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims] [S:jcc-form-ej-130] [S:jcc-form-ej-001]. Free small claims advisor help is available in each county [F:advisors].

Collection depends on having the debtor's correct legal name. California Courts warns that naming the defendant incorrectly can prevent the plaintiff from collecting after winning [F:defendant-name-collection]. This is one reason Chapter 3 spends time on identifying the right defendant.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Before choosing a collection tool, it can help to gather what you already know: the debtor's exact legal name, last known address, employer, and bank. Keep these notes with your case file and update them as you learn more.

## How long a judgment lasts

A California money judgment is generally enforceable for 10 years and can be renewed [F:judgment-ten-years]. For how and when to renew, check the official source [S:ccp-683-020].

## When the judgment is paid

When the judgment is paid in full, the judgment creditor must file an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (form SC-290) [F:satisfaction]. This tells the court the debt is paid. For the deadline and how to file it, check the form page [S:jcc-form-sc-290]. If you are the one who was paid, keep a copy of the filed form with your records.
