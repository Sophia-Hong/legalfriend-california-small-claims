# Planning for collection

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

A judgment is the court's decision. If the court decides money is owed, the court does not collect it for you. Many people only think about collection after the hearing. It can help to think about it before you file. This note is general information. It does not predict whether any judgment will be paid.

## Think about collection before filing

**The correct name.** California Courts warns that naming the defendant incorrectly can prevent the plaintiff from collecting after winning. [F:defendant-name-collection] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms] See the practice note "Naming the defendant correctly."

**A current address.** Keep the most recent address you have for the defendant, and note where it came from.

**Information you already have.** Sometimes you already know details from your normal dealings, such as the bank on a check the defendant gave you, or a workplace they told you about. Write down only what you already know from lawful, ordinary contact, with the source and date. This note does not suggest looking into anyone's private accounts or records.

**A realistic view.** A judgment is a court order, not a payment. Some judgments are paid quickly, some slowly, and some not at all.

## What happens after judgment

The clerk sends the parties a Notice of Entry of Judgment (form SC-130). [F:notice-of-entry] [S:jcc-form-sc-130] Keep it with your case papers.

A plaintiff cannot appeal a judgment on the plaintiff's own claim. A defendant can appeal, which leads to a new hearing in superior court. [F:plaintiff-no-appeal]

The winning party generally must wait until the time to appeal or to ask the court to vacate the judgment has passed before starting collection. [F:enforcement-wait] [S:ccp-116-810] Check the official source for the current timing.

## Statement of assets

Unless the judgment is paid or appealed, the losing party generally must complete a Judgment Debtor's Statement of Assets (form SC-133) and send it to the winning party within the time the law sets. [F:statement-of-assets] [S:jcc-form-sc-133] This form lists things like income and property. If it does not arrive, check the official guidance on what options exist. [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

## Common collection tools

Common collection tools include a writ of execution (EJ-130), used for bank levies and wage garnishment; an abstract of judgment (EJ-001), which creates a lien on real property; and an order for the debtor to appear for examination. [F:enforcement-tools] [S:jcc-form-ej-130] [S:jcc-form-ej-001]

A few plain-language terms:

- A "levy" takes money from a bank account to pay the judgment.
- A "wage garnishment" takes part of a paycheck.
- A "lien" is a legal claim attached to property.

Each tool has its own steps, forms, and costs. Check the official guidance before starting one. [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims] A free small claims advisor can explain the general steps. [F:advisors]

## How long a judgment lasts

A California money judgment is generally enforceable for 10 years and can be renewed. [F:judgment-ten-years] [S:ccp-683-020] Keep your case file for that whole time.

## When you are paid

When the judgment is paid in full, the judgment creditor must file an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (form SC-290). [F:satisfaction] [S:jcc-form-sc-290] The "judgment creditor" is the party owed money. Keep a record of every payment you receive, with dates and amounts.

## Common pitfalls

- Filing against a name that does not match the defendant's legal name.
- Starting collection before the waiting period has passed. [F:enforcement-wait]
- Not keeping a payment log, so the balance is unclear.
- Losing the Notice of Entry of Judgment.
- Forgetting to file SC-290 after full payment. [F:satisfaction]
- Letting the judgment period run without checking renewal. [F:judgment-ten-years]
- Contacting the other side in a way that feels pressuring. Keep contact brief, polite, and in writing.
