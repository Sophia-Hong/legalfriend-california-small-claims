# Choosing where to file

> DRAFT — AI-drafted for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

"Venue" means which court location hears the case. In California, that usually comes down to which county's superior court you file in. This note covers common mix-ups and how to keep a clear record of why you chose a court. It is general information, not advice about any specific case.

## What the rules look at

Small claims venue follows specific rules. Common places are where a defendant lives or does business, where a contract was made or was to be performed, or where the injury or property damage happened. There are special rules for certain consumer contracts. [F:venue-basics] Read the current statute and official guidance for the full list before you choose. [S:ccp-116-370] [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

This note does not add to those rules. It only points out where people often get confused.

## Common venue mix-ups

**"I'll file where I live."** Where you live is not, by itself, one of the common venue places listed above. [F:venue-basics] If you plan to file in your home county, check whether one of the listed reasons actually fits there. [S:ccp-116-370]

**Business address confusion.** A business may have a head office in one county, a store in another, and a mailing address in a third. Write down which address connects to your dispute, and why.

**Where the contract was "made."** With phone, email, and online deals, it is not always obvious where a contract was made or was to be performed. Note the facts you have, such as where work was done or where goods were delivered, and compare them to the official guidance. [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

**Consumer contracts.** Special rules apply to certain consumer contracts. [F:venue-basics] If your dispute involves one, read the statute rather than guessing. [S:ccp-116-370]

**More than one defendant in different counties.** When defendants live or do business in different places, the right court may not be obvious. A free small claims advisor can talk through general options. [F:advisors]

**Courthouse versus county.** Some counties have several courthouses, and not every one hears small claims. Check the court's website to find which location handles small claims filings.

## Check local rules and forms

Some courts require additional local forms, so the plaintiff should check the specific court's website or clerk. [F:local-forms] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms] Local practices can also differ on how papers are filed and which courthouse handles which area. This kit does not list local rules. Look them up on the court's own site.

## Document your reason on SC-100

A small claims case starts with form SC-100. [F:sc100-starts-case] When you fill it out, look for where the current form asks about the court you chose, and read the official instructions for that part. [S:jcc-form-sc-100] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

Practical steps that help:

- Write one or two plain sentences about why this court fits, based on the listed venue reasons.
- Keep a copy of what supports it, such as the defendant's business address, a contract showing where work was to be done, or photos of where damage happened.
- Save a dated screenshot of any business search that shows an address.

## Venue can affect service timing

Where the defendant is compared to where you file can matter for deadlines. Service must be completed at least 15 days before the hearing if the defendant is in the county where the case was filed, or at least 20 days before if the defendant is outside that county. [F:service-deadline]

## Common pitfalls

- Filing in your own county without checking whether a listed venue reason applies.
- Using a business's mailing address without checking whether it connects to the dispute.
- Skipping the court's website and missing a required local form. [F:local-forms]
- Going to a courthouse that does not handle small claims.
- Leaving the venue reason on SC-100 vague or blank.
- Forgetting that an out-of-county defendant changes the service timing. [F:service-deadline]
