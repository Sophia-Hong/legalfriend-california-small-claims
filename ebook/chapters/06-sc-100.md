# SC-100

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** A small claims case starts with one main form, SC-100. This chapter explains how to confirm you have the current version, the kinds of information the form asks for, and the other forms that may need to go with it. It describes the form in general terms. The form's own instructions control the exact items and wording.

## The form that starts the case

A small claims case starts with form SC-100, *Plaintiff's Claim and ORDER to Go to Small Claims Court*. [F:sc100-starts-case] [S:jcc-form-sc-100] The "plaintiff" is the person or business that files the case. The "defendant" is the person or business being sued.

## Confirming the current version

Court forms are updated from time to time, so it is worth confirming the version before filling one in. Whether a court accepts an older version is not covered in this kit; check with the court. [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

As observed on 2026-09-28, the California Courts SC-100 page listed the form as effective January 1, 2026. [F:sc100-effective] [S:jcc-form-sc-100] That may change after this kit was written. To confirm the current version:

- Open the official SC-100 page on the California Courts website. [S:jcc-form-sc-100]
- Note the effective date shown on that page.
- Compare it with the date shown on the copy you plan to use.
- Download a fresh copy from the official page if the dates do not match, or if you are not sure.
- Write down the date you checked.

Avoid copies from unofficial websites or old printouts.

California Courts also offers free form-filling help, including Guide & File, and free small claims advisors. [F:guide-and-file] [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]

## What the form asks for

The list below describes the *kinds* of information SC-100 asks for. It is not a line-by-line copy of the form. Item numbers, order, and wording can change between versions. Read each item on the current form and follow its instructions. [S:jcc-form-sc-100] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

**1. The parties.** The form asks for information about the plaintiff and each defendant, such as names and addresses. California Courts warns that naming the defendant incorrectly can prevent the plaintiff from collecting after a judgment in the plaintiff's favor. [F:defendant-name-collection] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms] Chapter 3 covers how to identify the right defendant.

**2. The amount and why it is owed.** The form asks how much is being claimed and a short explanation of why. Keep the explanation factual and brief. Your timeline from Chapter 5 can help. Keep the limits in mind: generally up to $12,500 for a natural person [F:limit-natural] [S:ccp-116-221], and $6,250 as the general limit for plaintiffs that are not natural persons. [F:limit-entity] [S:ccp-116-220] A claim may not be split into several cases to fit under the limit, and suing for less than the full amount generally means giving up the rest. [F:waive-excess]

**3. When it happened.** The form asks about the date or dates involved. Use the dates from your timeline.

**4. How the amount was calculated.** The form asks how the amount was figured. The itemized table from Chapter 5 is a useful starting point. Make sure the total on the form matches the total in your table.

**5. Whether you asked for payment.** The claim must state whether the plaintiff asked the defendant to pay before filing, or why not. SC-100 asks this question. [F:demand-first] [S:ccp-116-320] Your notes from Chapter 5 about the request and its date will help here.

**6. Why you are filing in this court.** The form asks about the reason the case is being filed in the chosen court. That reason ties back to the venue rules, which commonly look at where a defendant lives or does business, where a contract was made or was to be performed, or where injury or property damage happened, with special rules for certain consumer contracts. [F:venue-basics] [S:ccp-116-370] Chapter 4 covers this. Choose the reason on the form that matches your facts, following the form's instructions.

**7. Claims against a public entity.** The form asks whether the claim is against a public entity. Before suing a public entity, such as a city, county, state agency, or school district, a claimant usually must first present a government claim. This is generally within six months for injury or property damage and one year for other claims. The claimant may sue only after it is denied or deemed denied. [F:government-claim] [S:gov-911-2] [S:gov-945-4]

**8. The two-claims question.** A person may not file more than two small claims cases demanding more than $2,500 anywhere in California in the same calendar year. [F:two-claims-rule] [S:ccp-116-231] The form includes a question related to this. Count your earlier small claims filings for the year before answering.

**9. Fictitious business names.** A plaintiff doing business under a fictitious business name must file a declaration, form SC-103, showing compliance with the fictitious business name laws. [F:fbn-declaration] [S:ccp-116-430] [S:jcc-form-sc-103] A "fictitious business name" (often called a DBA, for "doing business as") is a name a business uses that differs from its legal name. The form asks about this.

**Signing.** Complete the signature and date areas as the form directs.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Before filling in the form, it helps to prepare a one-page worksheet with each topic above and your answer next to it. Then copy the answers onto the current form. This makes it easier to spot a missing piece of information before you get to the clerk's window.

## Attachments and extra forms

SC-100 may not be the only document needed. Depending on the facts, other forms can go with it.

- **More room for your explanation.** If an answer does not fit in the space provided, check the current form's instructions for how to add pages. [S:jcc-form-sc-100]
- **SC-100A, for more parties.** Form SC-100A lists additional plaintiffs or defendants when SC-100 does not have room for all of them. [F:sc100a] [S:jcc-form-sc-100a]
- **SC-103, for fictitious business names.** Filed by a plaintiff doing business under a fictitious business name, as described above. [F:fbn-declaration] [S:jcc-form-sc-103]
- **Local forms.** Some courts require additional local forms, so check the specific court's website or clerk. [F:local-forms] [S:cc-selfhelp-fill-out-forms]

The official small claims forms list shows the statewide forms. [S:cc-small-claims-forms] This kit does not list local forms for any county.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Before filing, read the finished form once from top to bottom as if you were seeing it for the first time. Check that names, dates, and amounts match your timeline and damages table. Small differences between documents are easy to miss and easy to fix at this stage.

If any item on the form is unclear, a small claims advisor can help. Each county provides free small claims advisor assistance. [F:advisors] [S:ccp-116-260]
