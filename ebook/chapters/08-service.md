# Service

> DRAFT — AI-drafted from sources/facts.yaml for attorney review. Not for release until approved.

**In this chapter:** After you file, the defendant has to be formally given copies of your papers. This step is called "service." This chapter explains who may serve, the common methods, the deadlines, how to prove service to the court, common mistakes, and what to look into if service cannot be finished in time.

## What service means

"Service" (also called "service of process") is the formal delivery of court papers to the person or business you are suing. It is how the defendant learns about the case and the hearing. Filing alone does not complete this step.

Which papers must be delivered, and in what form, is explained in the official small claims guidance. Check it before you give anything to your server [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

## Who may serve

You may not serve the papers yourself. Service must be done by one of these [F:service-who]:

- An adult (18 or older) who is not a party to the case.
- A registered process server.
- A sheriff or marshal.
- The court clerk, by certified mail.

A "party" is anyone named as a plaintiff or defendant. So a co-plaintiff cannot serve either. A friend, relative, or coworker who is 18 or older and not part of the case may be able to serve. Check the official source for the details [S:ccp-116-340].

## Common methods of service

The common service methods are [F:service-methods]:

- **Personal service.** The server hands the papers directly to the defendant.
- **Substituted service.** The server leaves the papers with a competent adult at the defendant's home or business, and then a copy is mailed.
- **Certified mail by the clerk.** The court clerk mails the papers by certified mail.

Each method has its own requirements. For example, the rules for substituted service include details about where the papers can be left and how the mailing is done [S:ccp-415-20]. If you plan to use certified mail by the clerk, ask the clerk how to request it, whether a fee applies, and how you will know whether it worked [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims].

If the defendant is a business, the rules on *which person* at the business can receive the papers matter. Check the official guidance before you choose who to serve [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims]. As a practical research step, the California Secretary of State's bizfile search can show a registered business's listed agent for service of process.

## Deadlines

Service must be completed at least 15 days before the hearing if the defendant is in the county where you filed. It must be completed at least 20 days before the hearing if the defendant is outside that county [F:service-deadline].

These deadlines are counted back from your hearing date. How days are counted (for example, how weekends and court holidays are treated) is set by law. Check the current rule on the official source before you rely on a date [S:ccp-116-340].

### Substituted service takes extra time

Substituted service is not complete until 10 days after the mailing, and those 10 days must be counted toward the service deadline [F:substituted-service-timing].

In practice, this means the papers must be left and mailed early enough that the 10 days pass *and* the 15-day or 20-day window is still met before the hearing. If your hearing is soon, substituted service may leave less room than you expect. Work out the dates on a calendar and confirm the counting rules on the official source [S:ccp-415-20].

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Start service as soon as your case is filed. Early service leaves time to try another method if the first attempt does not work. Give your server a short written sheet with the defendant's name, the service address, a description if you have one, your hearing date, and the last day service can be completed.

## Proof of service (SC-104)

After service, the server completes a proof of service, form SC-104. It is filed with the court before the hearing, by the deadline the court specifies [F:proof-of-service]. A proof of service is a signed statement telling the court who served which papers, on whom, how, and when.

The server, not you, fills out and signs the form. Read the official form and its instructions so you can give the server the right version [S:jcc-form-sc-104]. Ask the clerk what deadline your court uses for filing it.

> **LegalFriend practice note** — Before the server signs, review the draft SC-104 together. Check that the defendant's name matches your SC-100 and that the date, time, address, and method are filled in. Keep a copy of the filed proof of service in your case file (Chapter 9).

## Common mistakes

- **Serving the papers yourself.** A party may not serve [F:service-who]. This includes handing papers over "just to save time."
- **Missing the deadline.** Service completed fewer than 15 days (in county) or 20 days (out of county) before the hearing does not meet the deadline [F:service-deadline].
- **Forgetting the extra 10 days for substituted service.** The 10 days after mailing count toward the deadline [F:substituted-service-timing].
- **Using the wrong address.** An old home address, a closed business location, or a P.O. box may not reach the defendant. Confirm the address before you send your server out.
- **Not filing the proof of service.** Serving the papers is only part of the step. SC-104 must also be filed by the court's deadline [F:proof-of-service].

## If service cannot be completed in time

Sometimes a defendant is hard to find, or the hearing is too close to meet the deadline. If that happens, check the official guidance on what options exist [S:cc-selfhelp-small-claims], and ask the clerk.

One option to ask about is a postponement. A party can ask to postpone the hearing using form SC-150, and whether the request is granted is up to the court [F:postpone]. Read the official form for how and when to submit it [S:jcc-form-sc-150]. This kit does not say what happens at a hearing if service was not completed. Check the official source or ask the clerk.

Free help is available. Each county provides free small claims advisor assistance [F:advisors], and an advisor can walk you through service questions.
